Few phrases break my heart, legitimately, quite like hearing someone say: “I am not creative.” It’s often delivered as if they weren’t born with some arcane power of creativity and will therefore never be creative. It’s a limiting mindset to hold. “I am not creative” is also usually said with such defeat in the tone that it makes it tragic: I tried but failed to be creative.

I’ve been there. Many creative pitches over the years have been met with snickers or clearly polite wave-offs. Or missed landings, like in high school when my class video project, a *Reservoir Dogs* parody, drew blank stares from a whole class that had never seen the original. Anyone who puts themselves out there creatively has experienced this.

To all who want to be, you get to decide if you’re creative. If you want to be: you are. If any part of you does, even deep down, want to be creative; or if at some point in your life you did, before something happened to change that; or if you were ridiculed out of feeling creative; or if your desire to be creative was taken from you instead of given up willingly: you are creative, and you get permission to explore being creative. Full stop. No one else gets a say; fuck ’em.

There is no universal label of “creative” or not. No threshold your idea can cross to become the lauded creative idea. No creativity popular vote. The judgment of “creative or not” is entirely subjective. And even then, the perception exists to the perceiver in refracted shades — an imperfect view. You get the same view, and it morphs over time.

That means everyone — including you — keeps score. And it’s like *Whose Line*: everything’s made up and the points don’t matter. You alone choose which scoreboard to pay attention to and what points, if any, to tally up. You could choose, like I do, to count the exercise of teasing out and capturing the creative thought as the point — and the whole point.

It doesn’t matter whether the resulting idea or creation is “good” or not (what even is that?). Rather, the exercise of letting those creative thoughts flow, seeing what they lead to, pulling the thread — that is creativity. So being creative is being willing to go through that process, to do a little trial and error with what comes out, and to just see what happens.

Often: junk comes out. Sometimes: a workable nugget comes — it may need some intentional work to get up to par with the “safer” ideas, but workable. Every so often: a pristine gemstone falls out — ready for immediate use. The gemstones get the notice; they get the adoration of “you’re so creative” following behind. They’re the exception, not the rule. (Honestly, they’re usually a little less fun than the workable nuggets to me.) But if you’re keeping score of only gemstones, it’ll feel rough: like you’re not creative. By my scoreboard, though, those junk ideas took as much creativity as the gemstones, or more.

My hope is that the next time you see a chance to let your creative mind bat a question or problem around, you take it. That you work off your own scoreboard — even once to try it out. You shouldn’t need to hear it, but I can recall how deeply those blank stares, the stifled laughs, the unending silence after the “ta-da” have sunk into my mind at different points in my life, so it bears saying: you’re still creative in my book.
