When the darkness surrounds, it takes you down.

That’s what echoes through your head as you walk towards the darkened forest. That’s all anyone would ever say about the woods. After all of the stories, all of the disappearances, all of the bloody tracks — no one would talk about the woods. You’d ask, they’d smile and brush you off, repeating the refrain: When the darkness surrounds, it takes you down.

You’ve been patted on the head one too many times. You have to know what the grown-ups won’t tell you about the woods, you’ve decided, no matter what it costs. So here you trudge, one step closer to oblivion at a time. Each step making the clamor of the village fade further away. Each step bringing the singular dark mass of the woods closer. You swear you hear a growl. Your feet freeze on their own. Prankster? Animal?

Stop it — it’s just words, stories; it’s just trees, wood. There’s nothing. Nothing but darkness and little furry woodland animals — and maybe a couple big furry woodland animals. Just the same as the woods you run through during the day. That same downed tree you have to choose to either bound over or slide beneath as you race home for dinner. The same swampy mud hole you have to avoid, the one that still has your shoe from that one time. It’s just the woods. The dark woods.

Now at the woods, passing the edge. Twigs snap underfoot. Leaves rustle and you clomp through them. Moonlight fades behind the trees with each step. When the darkness surrounds, it takes you down. Your lantern flickers the darkness away in streaks, ebbing and flowing like ocean waves. Ice-cold wind stabs your uncovered skin — you pull the jacket tighter. Rain flecks onto your cheeks, each stinging like frozen metal.

More footsteps — yours? Echoes? Two sets, now three sets of footsteps swirling in the darkness. Your eyes dart, heart tightens. Just woods. Snap behind you; turn — nothing. Silly imagination. Shadowy faces dance between trees as you walk.

Your steps crunch quicker. Just trees. You see a person — no, a shadow. A tap on your shoulder — bumped a tree. You recoil hard into another tree. You flurry like a bug. No more ahead, only around — where is the road?

Your foot plunges into mud; a familiar shoe lies underfoot. Wriggling, you pull your foot free with a burp of mud. Phew, both shoes still on.

Lantern falters, then recovers. You look up; faint moonlight outlines the imposing treeline. You stumble around. Turning and turning — nothing looks familiar, not even where you stand. You pause.

Still air. Drip-drip-drip of the sprinkling rain. Then —

Wind whooshes in, flowing all around you. You hear your name bellowed in one of the gusts. The wispy flame of the lantern is extinguished. You see no path, just trees. Footsteps now — not yours. Coming quicker with each step. Faster than your heart beats. Thump-thump-thump — you can’t tell which is which. When the darkness surrounds, it takes you down.

Your mind races while you stand still. It’s just woods, just dark. Twigs. Branches. Leaves. Wind. Bump. Crunch. Your head spins. You gulp all the air you can and run. All you hear is footsteps — yours, theirs, its? Sprinting forward.

You hit that fallen tree, run straight into it — thwack.  
When the darkness surrounds, it takes you down.  
You bounce backwards onto the ground.  
When the darkness surrounds, it takes you down.  
All you see is dark sky, silver clouds.  
When the darkness surrounds, it takes you down.  
The moon is suddenly blocked out by a tendrilled shadow.  
When the darkness surrounds, it — GRAAAAHH!
