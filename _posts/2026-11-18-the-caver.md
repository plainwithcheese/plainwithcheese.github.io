Velvet darkness.

The cave is pungent — wet death. And yet, the deepest recesses call to Caver the most. They compel him inward and downward, pulling him harder than the pushback from his wife and young daughter.

Dripping stalactites. Errant screeching of distant bats.

The cave floor reminds him of the floor around the tub at bath time. His mind hears the squeals of fleeting fear after a just-pulled plug echo through the caverns.

Caver shakes his head and pushes on. Sweat already pours; it’s going to be a long haul. The directional beam of his backpack-mounted flashlight summons shadowy villages across the expanse of jagged stone walls. Each shadow-home shifts and flickers continuously with Caver’s movement.

What beauty, though. Even the small sliver of the caverns he can see is awe-inspiring. What must the rest of the Earth look like just below the surface? Every once in a while, a patch of quartz catches his light. Brief twinkles in this underground night sky. You’re lost in the constellations.

Rocks crack, snap. Crumbling.

Then a long slide. Caver falls back onto the ground. He clutches whatever his fingers happen to grab. Holds tight. The rubber of his boots grinds down on the rocks. Gear clangs as it plunges down after Caver.

He stops sliding. Caver’s chest pounds. He feels each heartbeat in his eyes.

Big puffs of air. Rapid at first, but slowing down until he’s only slightly hyperventilating.

Caver’s eyes widen. He sees his family leaning out the front door waving goodbye as he left home for this caving trip. Then he sees a pit of darkness below the ledge he’s overhanging. The flashlight doesn’t even light to the bottom. Caver rolls back from the edge. Hand across the face, over the eyes. Breathing slows to sighing.

Too damn close. Caver rethinks his footing strategy. Too close.

After a few minutes, Caver nods to himself. He reaches up for a handhold and pulls himself to solid footing. A cracking noise to his right startles Caver. He snaps his head right to find the sound and his body follows. He steps his left foot over to balance — there’s no floor underneath it.

Caver feels his body tip, then the sensation of being upside down.

Velvet darkness.

Dust and air and pain and sound rush into Caver with a gasp. He reflexively grabs his leg. Moist — that’s not good. Looking down, he paws at it, but can’t quite make it out. He leans back with a groan and reaches for his backpack flashlight. Nothing. He strains to see if it flew off him on impact. Nothing. Caver sees his wife begging him not to go on this trip.

Caver lets out a quick sigh. Then he remembers. He pats his jacket pocket — bingo. Caver pulls out two unlit glow sticks, cracking both and giving them a shake. A faint green glow fills the cave floor. There are no exit corridors around; it looks to him like he’s fallen into a pit or crevice. Upwards, the green glow gives way to sharp black after about ten feet.

Dull pain starts to fuzz its way up his leg. Caver looks down and now sees why: a sharp end of his tibia pierces through his skin, with what looks like cracked shards of his fibula next to it. Dark green-red blood oozes all around the injury. He shifts his leg and his throat gets thick when he sees the bones sliding around through the wound as it moves. He collapses back and covers his face. He sees a princess bandage — his crying daughter — peeling the paper backing — her tears turn to a skeptical frown, then a small smile — she wipes her eyes — big hug. Green glow, blurred by his tears.

Big breath in. Stale cave air. Caver strains as he pushes himself back up to a seated position. Everything’s awash in green. He looks up and squints. Can’t see outside his glow-stick campfire. He grabs one of the sticks, shakes it for good luck, and throws it up as high as he can. Sharp pain on the follow-through.

The glow stick ticks against the rocks and tacks its way down the cave walls. He doesn’t see anywhere that might be a way out. Another throw. Another. Again. Nothing. Panting, he looks up again. He kisses the stick. Heaves it upwards: tick-tack-tack-ta— it comes to a rest. Caver now sees a ledge, maybe the one he fell from, up at least a hundred feet.

Caver groans as he shifts his weight to slowly bring himself to a stand. He immediately crumples back down to the knee of his good leg — puking a bit as he does. He stands again, eyes closed until the pain dulls. Daughter smiling on her first day of school. He opens his eyes. Looks around for any of the climbing gear he was carrying. Nothing — no backpack, no flashlight, no ropes.

He shuffle-drags his way over to the cave wall. He gropes at it for a handhold. His fingertips easily glide down the wall. It’s not smooth, but there aren’t any good, low holds. Caver works around the room, leaning against the wall and feeling for any first step up he can take. Tears start to flow after about a quarter of the circumference, his face caked in the wall’s dust. You almost can’t watch him. By the time he’s made it all the way around, and then some, Caver sobs quietly — enough to let it out, but not enough to hear himself.

Caver takes a half-step back, then throws himself up and at the wall. He stretches his arms all the way up and just grabs. His body hangs for a moment against the wall, but then his fingernails crack off in shards as he slides back down to the ground. He does it again. Again. Again, and another time. Glow sticks are fading. His fingertips are raw. He barely pays them any mind. He jumps again and holds. Pulls, pulls. You can’t see him anymore. He’s not on the ground. Not hanging on the wall. Your eyes trace the cave wall up to the dimming glow stick on the ledge. Caver lies on the ledge on his side, holding himself up on one arm. Catching his breath. He couldn’t have made it up there. There are no footholds, no power in his leg… it’s at least a hundred feet up. No, it couldn’t be.

Caver pushes up from his resting position, slowly recovering. He looks forward and sees the cave wall ahead. He looks down: cave floor, blood patch in the sand, glow stick dead. He looks up: the ledge on which he just rested. “No!” He hobbles to the wall and smacks it with a hand. He sobs into the stone. “You fuck! I was just up there!” Another smack. Uncharacteristically unkind. He falls, pushed backwards off his feet by your flinch. He scoops his broken leg into his hands and screams. Echoes harmonize with his bellowing. Wife’s hand on his shoulder.

With a resolute grunt, Caver pulls himself to his feet. Confident steps forward. He reaches upwards — his daughter looks upon a cushion mountain — and starts climbing — she takes her first step — Caver’s hand grabs rock hold — little hand squeezes cushion — Caver’s face pressed against the wall, broken-leg-foot weakly pushes its toe into a sliver of a hold. The little girl summits Mt. Couch Cushion. She celebrates with a dance. She leaps off the peak — and into Caver’s arms. He laughs and spins her around in the bright morning.

Glow stick fades out. It’s pitch black now. Caver’s breathing fills the cavern, coming from all directions. You hear the distinct rhythmic sound of boots wedging into stone.

But where is Caver? More breathing, more struggle. You can imagine your own face against stone, contorting, and your own body clenching to work its way up. Hold by hold. Inches at a time. Straining muscles. Pushed forward from within.

Happier breathing. Is he close? He couldn’t possibly reach that ledge, you think. Not with that broken leg — a toss-up even without it.

The thwap of flesh on stone. More sobbing.

Caver cheek-to-cheek with his grinning daughter — him making trumpet noises with his mouth. His wife laughing as they twirl. Mt. Couch Cushion blurry behind them. Spiraling light spills on their faces as they spin, laughing.

“Laughing!” Caver snorts. “I bet you’re sitting there laughing at me! I can feel you watching me flail!”

Silence hums in Caver’s ears.

“Come on. My pack should be down here somewhere. Right?” The sounds of Caver searching the floor with his fingertips. Slap-taps with his hand every now and then. Otherwise silence and panting. You realize he’s right.

The rustle of synthetic fibers and gear straps. Caver lets out a gleeful “Thank you!” The bag rattles as it’s uprighted. A button clicks.

Light floods the cavern. Rainbows splotch the walls, cast from the broken lens of the flashlight. Caver staggers back, wearing a big, dumb grin.

His eyes snap back to the bag. He unzips it and pulls a few items out, dropping them into a pile. He grabs the flashlight and discards the pack.

Caver sets the flashlight on a rock and starts rigging. After a couple of retied knots, Caver is ready to scale the wall. He picks up the light and looks all the way up the near-sheer rock face. There’s the ledge — way up there.

With a grunt, Caver pushes up and starts to climb. He places a piton into a crack in the rocks. The flashlight sputters out. Metal jingles. Caver hammers the piton. Stone gives way to metal.

Boot wedging. Shuffles and jingles. Slowly higher.

A sharp piton tinks against the stone. Hammering. Jingles rise.

A clatter.

“Shit!”

Then jingles lower. Jingles slowly rise back up.

Hypnotic boot wedging, metal clanks, rhythmic pounding. Then again. Again.

Droning — becoming one long note.

Pulsing in your brain.

Your eyes droop.

Clank-pound-pound-jingle-step-jingle-clank.

The noise fades in your mind.

Never make it.

You know it.

Nylon rope zings. A reverberating racket tumbles down to the cave floor.

Couch cushions topple over.

Velvet darkness.

And there he lies, in tattered clothing; you’re sure.

Right?
