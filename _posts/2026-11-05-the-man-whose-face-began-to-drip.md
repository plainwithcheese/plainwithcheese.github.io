I’ll give you folks the inside tip
’Bout the man whose face began to drip.

As a baby he was chunky.
A teen: fresh and spunky.

In college, he was top,
But thirty’s when it stopped.

His skin began to crumble,
Tear apart, and tumble.

He tied a mask across his face,
And hoped that all would stay in place.

Did his face begin to crack, sure.
It bent and then it fractured.

He strapped his head up in a belt,
But it’d just ooze and slowly melt.

Held the bone and flesh real tight,
But none of it would fit quite right.

His face was almost a liquid,
And this is what it did:

It dripped.
And dripped.

And drip-drip-dripped.

His head sloshed around, poor soul;
He now keeps it in a fishbowl.
