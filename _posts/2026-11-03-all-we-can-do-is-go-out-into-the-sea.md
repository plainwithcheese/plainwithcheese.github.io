A fisherman prepared his nets. His son joined him in his shack as he worked. The boy watched in wonder as his father’s callused hands traced the nets. Each time the fisherman found a snagged thread or hole, he would repair it with new cord and tight knots.

The boy mimicked his father, checking his own section of net and trying to mend it the same way. Lantern light flickered in the shack as the two worked.

The fisherman paused. The boy felt his father’s eyes watching his careful hands work. His small fingers tied new cord to a torn portion of net. Furrowing his brow, the boy asked, “How do I know if I’m tying tight enough?” The fisherman resumed working, his only answer a faint “mmhm.”

The fisherman loaded his net in the boat. The boy flitted behind him. The fisherman returned to the shack and inspected a thick rope line, checking each twist. The boy started at the other end of the rope. He hoped he was doing it right. When his father reached the section the boy had fixed, the fisherman ran his fingers over the rope and moved on. Raindrops began to patter on the tin roof of the fishing shack. Tic-tic-tac-tic.

The fisherman inhaled until no more air would fit, then slowly exhaled. The boy sensed his father was at peace; he rarely saw him this calm at home. Or maybe the boy had never slowed down enough to notice. They continued working. The fisherman coiled his rope with the same care that he showed the nets. The raindrops plumped — tac-tac-tac. “Do fish like the rain?” the boy asked. The fisherman chuckled.

The boy followed his father outside. Rain poured, streaming into the boy’s eyes. Wind gusted. “Papa!”

The fisherman cupped a hand to his ear and leaned closer.

“Papa! How are we going to do this? It’s storming!”

The fisherman patted his son’s back.

“Where will the fish be?” the boy asked.

The fisherman smiled: “All we can do is go out into the sea.”
